# Lifecycle Evidence Protocol

## Purpose

Lifecycle evidence prevents sustainability design from confusing surface improvements with real reductions in ecological pressure.

## Required questions

1. What lifecycle boundary is being used?
2. Which material, energy, water, land, waste, or emissions flows are included?
3. Which flows are excluded?
4. Where might impacts be displaced?
5. What rebound effects are plausible?
6. What labor, community, or equity harms could be hidden?
7. What data sources support the claim?
8. What uncertainty remains?
9. Who has reviewed the evidence?
10. What monitoring is needed after implementation?

## Suggested evidence layers

| Layer | Evidence | Risk if omitted |
|---|---|---|
| Material inputs | Bills of materials, supplier data, material intensity | Hidden extraction pressure |
| Energy use | Operational and embodied energy | Understated emissions |
| Transport | Logistics and reverse logistics | Rebound from movement |
| Use phase | Behavior, maintenance, durability | Unrealistic adoption assumptions |
| End of life | Repair, reuse, recycling, disposal | Circularity overclaim |
| Labor and equity | Worker burden, household costs, community exposure | Justice blind spots |
| Governance | Ownership, monitoring, accountability | Non-durable implementation |
