# Method Note: Design Thinking for Sustainability

This folder provides a transparent decision-support framework for comparing sustainability design concepts across human-centered, ecological, circular, equity, institutional, and implementation dimensions.

The central model is:

\[
V_i = w_u U_i + w_f F_i + w_e E_i + w_c C_i + w_q Q_i + w_d D_i - w_r R_i
\]

Where:

- **Usability** captures whether the intervention is understandable, accessible, and adoptable.
- **Feasibility** captures practical implementation conditions.
- **Ecological benefit** captures expected reduction in environmental pressure or improvement in resilience.
- **Circularity** captures material stewardship, repair, reuse, retention, and waste reduction.
- **Equity** captures distribution of benefits, burdens, access, trust, and justice.
- **Durability** captures whether the intervention can persist under routine institutional conditions.
- **Risk** captures unintended consequences, burden shifting, green gentrification, weak governance, rebound effects, or maintenance failure.

## Recommended use

Use this model to support:

- sustainability concept review;
- circular economy design;
- climate-adaptation service design;
- public-sector sustainability planning;
- green procurement review;
- lifecycle-evidence prioritization;
- transition pathway prototyping;
- implementation and scaling review;
- evaluation and governance conversations.

## Interpretation

A high score is not proof of sustainability impact. It should be interpreted alongside lifecycle evidence, ecological expertise, stakeholder research, community review, governance constraints, labor implications, implementation feasibility, and long-term monitoring.
