# Method Note: Co-Design and Participatory Design

This folder provides a transparent decision-support framework for evaluating participatory design processes across representation, accessibility, participant influence, trust, evidence quality, implementation accountability, decision impact, and ethical risk.

The central model is:

\[
Q_i = w_rR_i + w_aA_i + w_pP_i + w_tT_i + w_eE_i + w_mM_i + w_dD_i - w_xX_i
\]

Where:

- **Representation** captures whether relevant and affected groups are included.
- **Accessibility** captures access supports such as language, disability access, scheduling, compensation, and format.
- **Participant influence** captures whether participants shape framing, synthesis, concepts, testing, implementation, and governance.
- **Trust quality** captures safety, transparency, credibility, and relational conditions.
- **Evidence quality** captures the reliability and traceability of participatory evidence.
- **Implementation accountability** captures whether participation connects to owners, decisions, resources, and follow-up.
- **Decision impact** captures whether participation changes decisions.
- **Ethical risk** captures tokenism, extraction, access failure, power asymmetry, data misuse, and legitimacy laundering.

## Recommended use

Use this model to support:

- participation planning;
- design-research review;
- co-design workshop planning;
- stakeholder coverage analysis;
- community engagement review;
- participatory prototype learning;
- public-sector design accountability;
- AI governance participation;
- service design legitimacy review;
- institutional learning and implementation planning.

## Interpretation

A high participation-quality score is not proof of legitimacy. It should be interpreted alongside direct participant feedback, community review, access audit, compensation review, facilitation assessment, data governance, implementation evidence, and transparent reporting about what changed because of participation.
