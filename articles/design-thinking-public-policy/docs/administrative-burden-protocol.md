# Administrative Burden Protocol

## Purpose

Administrative burden analysis helps public institutions understand whether a policy is difficult to access, understand, comply with, maintain, or challenge.

## Burden categories

1. **Learning burden:** discovering a program, understanding eligibility, interpreting rules, and knowing next steps.
2. **Compliance burden:** forms, documents, appointments, deadlines, verification, and reporting.
3. **Psychological burden:** stigma, stress, fear, shame, uncertainty, or procedural intimidation.
4. **Digital burden:** device access, connectivity, accounts, authentication, password management, and digital literacy.
5. **Time burden:** waiting, travel, phone calls, repeated visits, and case follow-up.
6. **Appeal burden:** understanding decisions, correcting errors, contesting denials, and securing procedural rights.
7. **Staff burden:** hidden labor shifted to frontline staff or caseworkers.
8. **Community partner burden:** hidden labor shifted to nonprofits, legal aid, clinics, schools, or community organizations.

## Review questions

- Which burdens are necessary, proportionate, and legally justified?
- Which burdens are accidental or avoidable?
- Which groups carry the highest burden?
- Which burdens are shifted from agencies to residents, families, staff, or nonprofits?
- What evidence shows burden reduction?
- What evidence would show burden displacement?
