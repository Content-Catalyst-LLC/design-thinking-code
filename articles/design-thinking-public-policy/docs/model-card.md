# Model Card: Public Policy Design Pilot Model

## Model name

Design Thinking in Public Policy Pilot Model

## Intended use

The model supports transparent comparison of public-policy pilot concepts across accessibility, feasibility, legitimacy, equity, administrative-burden reduction, durability, risk, evidence strength, stakeholder coverage, participation quality, legal complexity, and implementation complexity.

## Not intended for

- Automated policy decisions.
- Replacement of legal review.
- Replacement of democratic accountability.
- Replacement of community participation.
- Replacement of civil-rights, accessibility, privacy, labor, or equity review.
- Treating model outputs as proof of policy effectiveness.
- Ignoring qualitative evidence or lived experience.

## Inputs

- Pilot name.
- Policy domain.
- Pilot type.
- Accessibility.
- Feasibility.
- Legitimacy.
- Equity.
- Administrative-burden reduction.
- Durability.
- Risk.
- Evidence quality.
- Stakeholder coverage.
- Legal complexity.
- Implementation complexity.
- Participation quality.
- Optional policy-learning data.
- Optional administrative-burden data.
- Optional risk-register data.

## Outputs

- Policy value.
- Evidence-adjusted value.
- Evidence strength.
- Implementation readiness.
- Public legitimacy index.
- Equity access index.
- Learning priority.
- Policy resilience.
- Scenario rankings.
- Monte Carlo rank stability.
- Bootstrap stability.
- Random-weight sensitivity.
- Policy-learning pathway summary.
- Administrative-burden summary.
- Risk-register priority.

## Limitations

The model cannot represent full political, legal, institutional, historical, or justice complexity. It is a structured decision-support tool that helps teams make assumptions visible.

## Responsible use

Use the model to support deliberation, not replace it. Treat uncertainty, weak stakeholder coverage, low participation quality, high burden, high legal complexity, and unstable rankings as reasons for further inquiry.
