# Method Note: Design Thinking in Public Policy

This folder provides a transparent decision-support framework for comparing public-policy pilot concepts across public-value, implementation, equity, legitimacy, burden, and risk dimensions.

The central model is:

\[
V_i = w_a A_i + w_f F_i + w_l L_i + w_e E_i + w_b B_i + w_d D_i - w_r R_i
\]

Where:

- **Accessibility** captures public legibility, access, usability, and the practical ability to navigate the policy.
- **Feasibility** captures implementability under institutional, legal, operational, and fiscal constraints.
- **Legitimacy** captures public trust, acceptability, procedural fairness, transparency, and accountability.
- **Equity** captures differential access, burden, protection, and outcome distribution.
- **Burden reduction** captures reduction in learning, compliance, psychological, digital, time, and appeal burdens.
- **Durability** captures whether the intervention can persist under ordinary government conditions.
- **Risk** captures unintended consequences, legal risk, equity harm, digital exclusion, procedural harm, and governance failure.

## Recommended use

Use this model to support:

- public-policy pilot review;
- service redesign for public programs;
- administrative-burden reduction;
- policy-lab decision support;
- benefits access redesign;
- public-health outreach;
- climate-adaptation grant access;
- housing and tenant-support systems;
- disability benefit redesign;
- public participation and legitimacy review;
- implementation and scaling review;
- evaluation and governance conversations.

## Interpretation

A high score is not proof of public value. It should be interpreted alongside legal review, democratic accountability, budget analysis, community participation, administrative data, qualitative field evidence, accessibility testing, civil-rights review, privacy assessment, and implementation capacity.
