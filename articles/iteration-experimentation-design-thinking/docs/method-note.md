# Method Note: Professional Experiment Portfolio Scoring

This folder provides a transparent decision-support framework for iteration and experimentation in design thinking.

The model compares possible experiments across four criteria:

\[
V_i = w_l L_i + w_u U_i + w_e E_i - w_r R_i
\]

Where:

- **Learning gain** captures how much the experiment can teach the team about the problem, solution, stakeholders, system, or implementation pathway.
- **Update flexibility** captures reversibility, ease of revision, and the ability to incorporate feedback after the experiment.
- **Expected improvement** captures the plausible improvement produced if the experiment succeeds or reveals a productive design direction.
- **Residual risk** captures unresolved ethical, operational, interpretive, scaling, or institutional risk.

A more granular risk index may be represented as:

\[
R_i = \lambda_H H_i + \lambda_O O_i + \lambda_I I_i + \lambda_S S_i
\]

Where:

- \(H_i\) = ethical or human-subject risk;
- \(O_i\) = operational risk;
- \(I_i\) = interpretive risk;
- \(S_i\) = scaling risk.

## Professional interpretation

The score is not a final answer. It is a structured prompt for judgment.

A strong professional experimentation process should ask:

- What does this experiment need to teach us?
- What assumption is being tested?
- What evidence would change the design direction?
- Who bears the burden of the experiment?
- What ethical safeguards are needed?
- What forms of failure would be useful?
- What forms of failure would be unacceptable?
- What would make the result non-generalizable?
- What should be revised after the experiment?

## Recommended use

Use this model to support:

- experiment portfolio selection;
- prototype planning;
- service-design pilots;
- public-sector innovation labs;
- healthcare pathway testing;
- UX research planning;
- organizational learning;
- responsible experimentation documentation.
