# Method Note: Professional Problem-Frame Scoring

This folder provides a transparent decision-support framework for problem framing in design thinking.

The model compares possible problem frames across four criteria:

\[
V_i = w_e E_i + w_s S_i + w_o O_i - w_r R_i
\]

Where:

- **Explanatory adequacy** captures how well a frame explains the observed evidence, stakeholder experience, behavioral pattern, institutional constraints, and system dynamics.
- **Stakeholder coverage** captures how well the frame includes affected groups, especially those who may be less visible to the institution.
- **Opportunity value** captures whether the frame opens useful downstream design questions, prototypes, or interventions.
- **Framing risk** captures over-narrowness, stakeholder exclusion, misplaced causality, political distortion, false actionability, or institutional convenience.

A more granular risk index may be represented as:

\[
R_i = \lambda_N N_i + \lambda_X X_i + \lambda_C C_i + \lambda_P P_i
\]

Where:

- \(N_i\) = narrowness risk;
- \(X_i\) = stakeholder exclusion risk;
- \(C_i\) = causality risk;
- \(P_i\) = political distortion risk.

## Professional interpretation

The score is not a final answer. It is a structured prompt for judgment.

A strong professional framing process should ask:

- Who proposed the frame?
- Whose interests does the frame protect?
- What evidence supports the frame?
- What evidence would falsify or weaken the frame?
- Which stakeholders become central under this frame?
- Which stakeholders disappear?
- What solutions become imaginable?
- What solutions become excluded?
- What counterframes must be tested before committing?

## Recommended use

Use this model to support:

- early-stage design research synthesis;
- public-sector problem definition;
- innovation strategy;
- service design;
- systems mapping;
- counterframe review;
- prototype prioritization;
- frame-risk documentation.
